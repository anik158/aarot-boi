# How to convince a rice shop owner to use AratBoi

This is a script for you to speak from. Do not print it and hand it to the owner. He will not read a pitch. He will watch your hands, look at four numbers, and decide in a few minutes whether the phone is safer than the red notebook he already trusts.

He is usually 40 to 65. He reads slowly. He has run the shop on paper for decades. He has already seen apps that needed internet, a training session, or a young person standing next to him. Those apps made him feel dependent. This conversation has to do the opposite: show him one mess he already hates, then show that one sale cleans that mess without taking his old book away.

Do not open with “digital ledger,” “4-in-1 automation,” or a tour of the app. Those words tell him the product was built for someone younger. Open with the fight he already has every night, when four handwritten books refuse to agree.

## Who you are talking to

Talk to the owner, not the boy who writes the book. The boy may be faster on a phone. The owner is the one who loses money if a number is wrong, and he is the one who will refuse the app if he cannot check it himself.

He may not type fast. That is fine. His son can install the app. After that, the owner only needs to read four big numbers and watch one sale get saved. If those four numbers make sense, he will keep using it. If the first screen is busy, he will decide the app is for his nephew and put the phone down.

What he is afraid of:

- A wrong number that stays hidden until a mill owner or a buyer argues with him. By then the mistake is public, and he looks careless in his own market.
- An app that stops when the market wifi stops. Rice markets often have bad signal. A tool that dies at the busiest hour is useless.
- Looking foolish in front of his staff because he cannot find the right button. Pride matters as much as money here.
- The paper book and the phone saying different totals. Then he trusts neither, and the phone becomes one more book he has to reconcile at night.

What he already respects, so use these and nothing fancier:

- One clear number he can point at with a finger.
- The words he already uses. You will say them in Bangla. Here is what they mean, so you know you are saying the right thing:
  - **বস্তা (bosta)** — a sack of rice. This is how he counts stock, not kilograms first.
  - **বাকি (baki)** — money still unpaid. When a buyer owes the shop, that is baki.
  - **মিলার (miller)** — the rice mill that sent the rice. On commission rice, the shop owes the mill after the sale.
  - **কমিশন (commission)** — the shop’s fee for selling rice that still belongs to the mill.
  - **নগদ (nogod)** — paper cash in the drawer. Not bKash, not Nagad.
  - **খাতা (khata)** — a handwritten account book.
  - **চালান (chalan)** — the invoice for one load of rice leaving the shop.
  - **খালাসি (khalasi)** — the laborers who unload the sacks. Their pay is one lump sum, not a rate per sack.
- A phone that still works when the network is gone. The app saves on the phone itself. Internet is not required to cut an invoice.

## The problems he already has

He does not think he has a software problem. If you describe “features,” he will nod and forget you. He thinks he has the five problems below. Name the problem the way he feels it, then show the screen that answers it. Do not name a feature first.

### 1. The same sale is written four times

One truck of rice creates four different records:

- How many sacks left the warehouse.
- How much that buyer still owes the shop.
- How much the shop still owes the mill, if the rice was the mill’s.
- How much paper cash should be in the drawer, if he was paid in cash.

A clerk writes these in four books, often at different moments, often with one line missed. At night the owner sits down to make the four books match. They do not. He stays late hunting the missing line. That is the pain. The app’s answer is not “digital.” The answer is: type the sale once, and those four records move together, because they are the same entry.

### 2. Commission rice is where the fights start

A lot of the rice in his warehouse is not his. The mill sent it. He sells it for the mill, keeps a fee per sack, pays the unloaders, and owes the mill whatever is left. On paper that sum is easy to get wrong, and the mill does not forgive a wrong net.

Say the sum in this order, because this is the order he already calculates:

1. Sacks × selling price = the sale total.
2. Subtract the commission (fee per sack × number of sacks). That fee stays with the shop.
3. Subtract the labor lump. Labor is one total for the whole load, including truck fare if he folded it in. There is no separate truck-fare box.
4. What remains is what the mill should receive. The app floors this at zero, so a typing mistake cannot show the mill owing the shop.

A concrete example you can write on his counter if he wants numbers:

- 200 sacks × ৳3,000 = ৳6,00,000 sale.
- Commission ৳20 per sack × 200 = ৳4,000 kept by the shop.
- Labor lump ৳13,600 paid out in cash.
- Mill should get ৳6,00,000 − ৳4,000 − ৳13,600 = ৳5,82,400.

Two things owners mix up, so say them before he asks:

- If the buyer does not pay the full sale today, the buyer owes the shop. That unpaid part does **not** reduce what the shop owes the mill. The mill’s number and the buyer’s number are separate.
- If the rice is his own stock, the mill is not billed at all. Own rice and commission rice must be chosen before typing, because they write different books.

### 3. He cannot see who owes him without opening a book

A buyer walks in. The owner wants the due in one look, then a phone call or a WhatsApp chase. Searching a paper book in a loud market is slow, and it is embarrassing if he cannot find the page while the buyer is standing there. The app keeps a balance on each person: positive means that person owes the shop, negative means the shop owes that person (usually a mill).

### 4. The cash box and the book disagree

At closing he counts notes. If the book says one total and the drawer says another, he does not know whether a sale was written wrong or someone took cash. The app’s cash number is only paper cash: cash received minus cash paid. bKash, Nagad, and bank transfer are recorded so he can see them, but they do not change the drawer number. If you blur that, he will compare the phone to a drawer that can never match, and he will throw the phone out the same night.

### 5. His eyes and his patience are limited

Small text, a dark glowing screen, and five buttons that do the same job make him put the phone down and go back to the red book. He will not explore. The home screen is four equal boxes and one green button, **নতুন চালান** (“new invoice”). The sales chart is hidden until he taps today’s sales. Leave it hidden during the first look. A graph on arrival feels like a gadget. A graph after he asks “how was this week?” feels like an answer.

## What you say, in his words

Stand at the counter. Do not demo every screen. Show Home, then one sale, then the same Home so he can see the numbers move.

**Open with this, out loud:**

> সন্ধ্যায় চারটা খাতা মেলাতে হয়। একটা চালানে চারটা খাতা নিজে আপডেট হয়ে যাবে। ইন্টারনেট না থাকলেও চলবে।

Meaning, slowly: “At night you have to make four books match. One invoice will update all four by itself. It still works when there is no internet.”

Then stop talking and show only the four boxes. Point at each one. Do not scroll yet.

| Box on the phone | Plain meaning | What you say in Bangla | What that Bangla means |
|---|---|---|---|
| মোট মজুদ চাল | Sacks in the warehouse right now | গোডাউনে এখন কত বস্তা। বই খুলতে হবে না। | “How many sacks are in the godown now. You do not have to open the book.” |
| আজকের মোট বিক্রি | Today’s sales in taka | আজ কত টাকার বিক্রি। চাপ দিলে সাত দিনের ছবি। | “How much you sold today. Press it and you see the last seven days.” Only say the second sentence if he asks. Do not open the chart yourself. |
| বাজারে মোট বাকি | Total money buyers still owe the shop | বাজারে আপনার কত টাকা বাকি পড়ে আছে। | “How much of your money is still sitting out in the market.” |
| ক্যাশ বাক্সে নগদ | Paper cash that should be in the drawer | ড্রয়ারে কত নগদ থাকার কথা। | “How much paper cash the drawer is supposed to hold.” |

If he nods at “বাজারে মোট বাকি,” stay there. That is the number that hurts him, because it is money he has already sold but has not collected. Do not rush on to stock or settings just because you prepared more.

## Match each fear to one proof

Do not list features. He hears a list as a sales pitch. Answer the sentence he actually says, then prove that one sentence on the phone.

**He says the books do not match** (“আমার খাতায় গরমিল হয়”).

Do one sale. Then go back to Home and point at the boxes that changed: sacks went down, today’s sales went up, the buyer’s due went up by the unpaid part, and the cash box went up only by the cash he received. Say that the books cannot forget a line, because there is only one line. The four numbers are four views of that same line.

**He says commission math starts fights** (“কমিশনের হিসাব ঝগড়া বাধায়”).

Open a commission invoice, not an own-stock one. The two choices are large cards on purpose: **নিজস্ব চাল** means “my own rice,” and **কমিশন চালান** means “the mill’s rice, I only earn a fee.” Say this out loud:

> এটা আপনার চাল না। মিলারের চাল। বিক্রি থেকে কমিশন আর খালাসি বাদ দিলে মিলার যা পাবে, সেটা এখানে।

Meaning: “This rice is not yours. It is the mill’s. Take the sale, subtract your commission and the labor, and what is left is what the mill gets. It is shown here.”

Use his own sacks and prices if he offers them. The mill’s net should be visible before he taps save, so he can argue with the number while you are still there, not after the mill calls him. If this load is his own rice, switch to the other card and say the mill is not billed, because that rice is already his.

**He says he cannot collect what he is owed** (“বাকি আদায় করতে পারি না”).

Open one buyer. Show the balance. Tap call, or the WhatsApp reminder. The point is not WhatsApp. The point is he does not have to hunt a page while the buyer is standing in front of him. The number is already on the person.

**He says the cash never matches** (“ক্যাশ মিলে না”).

Show the cash-box number, then the note count (how many 1000, 500, 200 notes, and so on). Tell him clearly: counting the notes does not change the book. It only tells him whether the drawer matches the book. That is the night check he already does with a pen. Also tell him bKash is not in that number. If today’s collection was on the phone wallet, the drawer will not go up, and that is correct.

**He says there is no internet** (“ইন্টারনেট নেই”).

Turn mobile data off in front of him. Cut one invoice. Save it. Turn data back on only if you want to show that nothing was waiting to upload. Say the market wifi is not required. The sale is stored on this phone.

**He says he cannot read it, or there are too many buttons** (“চোখে ধরে না”).

Stay on Home. Four boxes, one green button. Do not open settings, license, or shop profile unless he asks. If you open them, you are proving his fear.

## How to run a 10-minute trial

Agree on a real sale he is about to write in the red book. Do that one sale in the app beside the book. Do not offer to type in his whole year. A full migration is how you lose an evening and his trust. One sale he can check by eye is enough.

1. Before typing anything, choose own rice or commission rice, and say which one it is in Bangla. If you guess wrong, the wrong book moves, and the trial fails even though the addition is correct.
2. Enter sacks, price, and the buyer. If the buyer is not in the list, add him from the same dropdown. Do not leave the screen to go create people first. Leaving the screen feels like work.
3. Enter what was paid now, and whether it was paper cash or bKash. Say out loud that only paper cash changes the drawer. bKash is kept in the payment list so he can see it later. It is not in the note box.
4. Save.
5. Look at the four boxes and at that buyer’s balance. Read them against the line he was about to write in the red book.

If those match, stop talking. Do not show another screen to “be complete.” Leave the app with him for the rest of the day, and only for new sales. The old paper stays the master until he asks to leave it. You are asking him to run a parallel line for one day, not to abandon the book that has kept the shop alive.

## What not to promise

Saying any of these will blow up the first evening he checks, because the app does not do them.

- Do not say it replaces his accountant on day one. It stops the four books drifting apart. It does not file his tax or argue with the mill for him.
- Do not say the credit limit blocks a sale. The field **সর্বোচ্চ বাকি** means “maximum due.” Zero means no limit. If a buyer goes past the number, the app shows a warning. He can still sell. Owners hate a tool that refuses a sale while a buyer is waiting.
- Do not call empty sacks money. **খালি বস্তা** means empty jute sacks the buyer still has to return. They are a count of bags, not taka. If you call them money, tonight’s cash check will look wrong.
- Do not promise a live license countdown, or that the phone locks after 30 days. The screen can say the app is active. That is a label, not a lock. The real promise is smaller and true: it works with no internet.
- Do not show English on his screen, do not open the seven-day chart before he taps today’s sales, and do not point at two buttons that both mean “new invoice.” There is one: the green **নতুন চালান** button.

## If he says no

He is not rejecting you. He is protecting the book that has kept the shop alive. Do not argue, and do not offer a discount on a promise. Offer a test he can cancel himself, then leave:

> আজ একটা চালান। বাকি খাতা আগের মতো। সন্ধ্যায় দুইটা যোগফল মিলিয়ে দেখবেন। না মিললে ফোন রেখে দেবেন।

Meaning: “Just today’s one invoice. The rest of the books stay as they are. Tonight, compare the two totals. If they do not match, put the phone away.”

That close works because it respects his age and his risk. He keeps the red book. He risks one invoice, not the shop. He is the one who decides at night, with nobody standing over him.
